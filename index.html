About

I turn complex, high-dimensional data into decisions. As a PhD scientist, I have spent years analyzing large-scale biological datasets — single-cell and spatial transcriptomics measuring thousands of genes across diverse cell populations — to answer hard questions about how cancers resist therapy.

My toolkit is fundamentally analytical: statistical analysis, clustering and dimensionality reduction, differential testing, pathway enrichment, and trajectory inference, implemented in R and Python on Linux HPC clusters with Git-based version control. I design analyses end to end — from framing the question, through data processing and quality control, to communicating results to collaborators from other disciplines.

As a postdoctoral fellow at NYU Grossman School of Medicine, I own projects from experimental design through analysis and interpretation, work across disciplines, and mentor junior scientists. I am now bringing this analytical background to industry — data analytics, data science, and quantitative roles where rigorous analysis of complex data drives real decisions.
